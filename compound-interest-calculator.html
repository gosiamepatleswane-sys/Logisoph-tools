How Compound Interest Works

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money and the accumulated interest from previous periods. Instead of growing in a straight line, your savings accelerate over time because each month's return generates its own returns in future months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Rule of 72?
The Rule of 72 is a quick way to estimate how many years it takes to double your money: divide 72 by your annual interest rate. For example, at an 8% annual return, your money doubles roughly every 9 years (72 ÷ 8 = 9).

Why does starting early matter so much?
Even small monthly deposits grow substantially over 15 to 30 years because compound growth does most of the heavy lifting in the later years. In South Africa, Tax-Free Savings Accounts (TFSAs) and low-cost index funds allow investors to keep 100% of their compound returns tax-free up to annual limits.
